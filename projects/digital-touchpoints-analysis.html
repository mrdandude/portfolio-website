Problem

Churn was not one problem. It was a collection of value and friction signals.

Looking at churn only as a final outcome hides the customer behaviors that lead to it. I organized qualitative reasons by customer segment and period to understand which signals reflected a value gap, an operational obstacle, or fading product relevance.

01

Value gap

Customers questioned cost, ROI, or whether the product delivered enough ongoing value.

02

Operational friction

Billing, account access, and administrative issues created preventable reasons to leave.

03

Relevance erosion

Low usage, duplicate accounts, and completed projects weakened product stickiness.

Patterns

Different customer groups needed different retention conversations.

Higher-value segment

Prove strategic value.

Churn signals centered on ROI, price sensitivity, competitive alternatives, and changing business priorities. Retention messaging needed to connect product activity to outcomes.

  • Lead with personalized impact
  • Frame value against business risk
  • Surface signals before renewal
Lower-usage segment

Reduce friction and rebuild habit.

Churn signals centered on limited usage, account duplication, billing issues, and easier alternatives. Retention needed to make the product simpler to use and easier to remember.

  • Resolve account friction quickly
  • Reinforce useful product moments
  • Create a consistent value cadence

Behavioral strategy

Make value easier to process, organize, and recall.

01

Levels of processing

Replace shallow feature reminders with personally meaningful outcomes, such as time saved, incidents resolved, or risk reduced. Specific relevance creates deeper memory.

From "Here are our features" to "Here is what your team accomplished."

02

Organization effect

Group customer outcomes into a small, repeatable framework so value is easier to scan and retrieve: cost savings, risk reduction, and team efficiency.

A consistent structure reduces the effort required to understand value.

03

Mere exposure

Reinforce the same core value themes across dashboards, lifecycle messages, and account conversations instead of waiting for a renewal or risk event.

Frequent, low-friction reminders build familiarity over time.

Reflection

Retention begins before a customer becomes a churn risk.

The strongest insight was that churn messaging should not begin at renewal. Customers need a repeated, personalized understanding of value throughout the relationship.

If I continued this work, I would validate the concepts through customer interviews and test message cadence, metric relevance, and behavioral response across customer segments.
